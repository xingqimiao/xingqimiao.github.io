title: 'Take part in the 2026 Survey on the Living Conditions of Transgender People in China'
---

We are running an anonymous survey:

**the 2026 Survey on the Living Conditions of Transgender People in China.**

It is meant to record what life is actually like for transgender and gender-diverse people in China, covering — among other things:

- basic living and economic circumstances
- gender identity and expression
- access to healthcare and HRT experience
- family relationships and guardianship
- education
- employment and working conditions
- mental health and quality of life
- community support and social environment

No questionnaire can hold an entire life. Trans people's situations are complicated: some have lived in their gender for years, others are still deep in the closet; some are on HRT or have had surgery, others can't start because of family, money, age, geography or safety; some are up against a lack of medical resources, others against family pressure, hiring limits, school rules, platform policies, or structural pressure much larger than any one person.

So this survey is not here to label anyone, or to decide who is "more typical". We want the widest possible range of real experience, so the report doesn't only reflect the people who are easiest to see.

## What you should know before filling it in

The survey is anonymous. We will never ask for your name, phone number, ID number, email address or other directly identifying information.

It does ask about medical care, family, mental health and experiences of discrimination. If you don't want to answer something, there is "not applicable" and "prefer not to answer", and you can skip whole sections. If it starts to feel like too much, please stop — that comes first.

This is not a diagnosis or medical advice. Every question about HRT, surgery, mental health or diagnosis certificates exists only to understand the community's situation.

## How the data is used

Responses feed anonymous statistics, cross-analysis and public reports. What gets published is aggregate data, trend analysis or de-identified excerpts — never a single respondent's complete original questionnaire.

Because the questionnaire was revised several times after collection started, we keep data by version. Older responses are never deleted or overwritten. Where a question, an option or the skip logic changed, we don't force the versions together; the report explains the difference.

The current version is:

**P1.3 / 2026-06-03 (revision of medical barriers, information sources, online-seller wording and support-resource definitions)**

## On ethics review

The survey is run by KiraMyao Equal as a community initiative. Its purpose is to record the real living conditions of trans people in China and to give public communication, community support and advocacy a factual footing.

It has not been reviewed by a university, hospital or other institutional review board. It therefore should not be read as formal medical research, and it does not constitute medical, diagnostic or treatment advice.

We try to follow the principles of voluntary participation, skippability, anonymity, minimal harm and transparency, and the final report states the sample's origin, recruitment method, version differences, data limits and likely biases.

## Why we hope you'll fill it in

Discussion about trans people in China has long lacked systematic records from the community itself. We want this survey to show, concretely:

- where medical care actually gets stuck
- how family support and family pressure change over time
- what structural difficulties exist at school and at work
- how experiences differ by city, age and income
- where psychological pressure comes from
- whether community support actually reaches the people who need it

Data doesn't change reality on its own, but it can become the basis for reports, advocacy, mutual aid and public communication.

## Please take part — and pass it on

If you're willing, please fill it in.  
If you know someone who is trans, non-binary, genderqueer, still exploring their gender, or has related experience, please forward it to them.

We especially want to reach people who rarely appear in public community spaces: people deep in the closet, students, people already working, people in different cities and regions, people at different stages of medical transition, people with no HRT or surgery experience, and non-binary and gender-diverse people.

Your experience does not have to be "typical enough" to deserve recording.  
If it is real, it is worth being seen.
