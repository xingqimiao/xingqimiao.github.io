title: '"I''m a cat now" — a story about SRS'
---

Today I was only looking things up:
if SRS can't be done in China, could we still go abroad?

Then I hit a blog post that stopped me for a long time 😖:

> "Off to Thailand for the cat-transformation surgery…"
> "I'm a cat now!"

![c397b1f7-89a2-47f8-bc80-d295fb7a3e88](/pic/blog/becoming-a-cat-a-story-about-srs-01.png)

Reading those lines, I thought she was adorable — as if she were off to fulfil a small fairytale wish, finally slipping away from the crowd to become a free little cat.

And then, while still smiling, my eyes stopped focusing. My chest tightened and the tears came hot.

Because we know what that airy little phrase **cat-transformation surgery** actually means.
It means anaesthetic, scalpels, a long recovery.
It means pain, weakness, infection risk, and nights in a hospital room in a foreign country.
It means telling yourself, again and again, right before it starts:
*I can't regret this.*
*I've come this far.*
*I'm going to finish the thing I've wanted for more than ten years.*

She wrote it so lightly.
"I'm a cat now."

But what I hear is someone steadying herself.
As if calling it "cat surgery" would make the pain a little lighter.
As if saying "I'm a cat now" could gently cover up the loneliness, the fear, the bleeding, and all those nights nobody understood.

But…
**None of this should have to be carried alone.**

Why does someone who only wants to be themselves first have to be put on trial by their family, filtered by the system, worn down by money and tested by pain?
For a lot of trans people, being twenty means studying and working part-time at once, saving for surgery yuan by yuan, breaking down late at night certain they can't go on —
and then waking up the next morning, wiping their eyes, and going to work anyway.

Because there is only one thought in their head:
**I want to live as myself.**

Just to have one day when they can finally use their own body to start their own life,
and then, to that disgusting fate, toss out — lightly, stubbornly, adorably:

*"I'm a cat now…"*

A hug to every cat who has made it this far. You are all so brave.
May every cat not only survive from here on,
**but actually start living…**
