---
title: 'Assessment Report on Transgender Healthcare in China'
---

###### The current transgender healthcare system in China is still built on a "pathologizing" classification, layered with procedural and social barriers that leave transgender people facing severe obstacles to gender-affirming care and legal gender recognition.

### 🧩1 Pathologizing of transgender people

- China still classifies transgender people under "transsexualism" (易性症), a category of mental disorder (CCMD-3).

- The 2009 *Technical Management Specification for Sex Reassignment Surgery* explicitly calls transgender people "transsexual patients".

- Although the 2017 revision replaced the wording with "gender reassignment technology" and dropped the term "patient", the pathologizing framework remains unchanged.

- This is clearly out of step with international trends (e.g. WHO's depathologization in ICD-11, 2018).

- The consequences of pathologization:

- Heavy prerequisites before medical care

- Deepened social stigma

- Legal gender recognition made conditional on "psychiatric diagnosis + surgery"

## 🧩2 Requirements for gender-affirming surgery

The 2022 *Technical Management Specification for Gender Reassignment Technology* requires applicants to provide:

### Documents that must be submitted

- A certificate of no criminal record issued by public security authorities.

- A diagnosis of transsexualism (gender dysphoria or gender incongruence) issued by a psychiatrist or psychologist at a tertiary (Level 3) hospital.

- A written application by the person themselves, notarized (while notaries may also challenge the applicant).

- Proof that immediate family members have been informed (in practice equivalent to "family consent").

### Conditions that must be met

- Persistent gender transition intention for at least 5 years.

- Unmarried.

- Age > 18 (the age of majority in China).

- No contraindication to surgery.

### Real-world impact

These conditions create huge barriers, for example:

- Family disapproval → no surgery → some people have died by suicide (documented cases)

- Sex workers cannot prove a clean criminal record → no surgery

- The marriage requirement forces married transgender people to divorce first

- The age limit excludes many minors with urgent medical needs

## 🧩 3 Medical qualifications for gender reassignment surgery

- In practice, extremely few hospitals in China perform these surgeries, and their fees are very high.

## 🧩 4 Missing care standards

- Although hospitals must be qualified, there are no professional care standards for transgender healthcare.

- Doctors receive no training on transgender issues, resulting in:

- Misdiagnosis

- Forced "conversion"

- Inappropriate psychological intervention

- Medical discrimination

## 🧩 5 Medical costs

- Gender-affirming surgery and hormone therapy are not covered by medical insurance (except Progynova, an estradiol medication).

- Surgery is expensive.

- Financial pressure forces many transgender people to:

- Give up surgery

- Seek surgery overseas

- Resort to unregulated, informal or black-market surgery (extremely high risk)

![Figure 2: Financial burden of gender-affirming care](/pic/report/china-transgender-healthcare-assessment-report-02.en.png)

#### ⚠Core problem: China's transgender healthcare system is built on an institutional structure of "pathologization + high barriers + family control + high costs".

The consequences:

- Extremely low accessibility of medical care

- Legal gender recognition tied to "surgery first"

- Institutionalized family and social pressure

- Outward spillover of medical risk (self-harm and suicide, overseas surgery, black-market surgery)
