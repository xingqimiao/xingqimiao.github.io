---
title: Fluid Shapes
---

I actually came across the concept of "transgender" quite early, but at the time I didn't explore it deeply. Since childhood I've wished and yearned to be a boy, even though everyone told me "you're a girl" based on my assigned sex. The thought stayed buried inside, never surfacing strongly, and I didn't feel much discomfort or anxiety about it.

The first sharp memory is elementary school, when I cut my hair short. I was especially happy that time; when people took me for a boy, I never explained myself. I just felt inside, "I guess it doesn't matter." In middle school, at puberty, everyone's secondary sex characteristics began to develop — the boys' voices changed, the girls' bodies changed — but I clearly grew anxious, especially because I was a bit early-blooming: breast development made me deeply resistant. I would buy chest binders online and flatten my chest, completely unlike other girls my age, who accepted their body changes.

For a while I thought I might be a "butch T" among lesbians — a masculine-dressing lesbian. But later I felt that wasn't right either: I had no particularly strong interest in women, and men seemed fine too. So was I bisexual? That question occupied my thoughts for a long time. By high school, I honestly wasn't that troubled by it, though occasionally I'd still be mistaken for a boy — but not often, since my voice is still fairly thin. Meanwhile, I also took part in feminist activities and threw myself into them.

What really made me see myself clearly came after I connected with some communities. I began to realize that, from childhood, my inner identification was actually male — purely male. Yet I also feel gender identity can be fluid. Over the years, with few negative external influences, I gradually accepted the parts of myself assigned female, but that doesn't mean I fully identify as a cisgender woman. So at this stage, my gender identity leans more toward "non-binary" and "agender" — all still in exploration.

As for my sexual orientation, my preferences seem to shift frequently; to this day I'm not sure which gender or type of person I prefer. Now, with the help of public-good organizations and communities, I've started HRT. But my goal isn't a complete "gender correction". One reason is that accessing medication in the current Chinese medical environment is genuinely difficult; another is that I'm not particularly distressed; and my position as non-binary means I won't consider full surgical transition for now — though I might consider chest-flattening surgery.
