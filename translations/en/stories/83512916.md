As a child I was very outgoing and loved talking to people; from a very young age, the whole compound near my grandmother's place knew me 😰 And... a bit of "manly pride"? I'd go around declaring "men are great" all day, feeling very positive-energy. I had no idea what it meant; I only knew it made my family happy.

In kindergarten, I was separated from my parents for the first long period, and like any kid I bawled my eyes out () Naturally. For those kindergarten years, I somehow have no memory at all — completely gone now. So let's skip kindergarten (what a "skip-the-boring-bits" moment).

In grades 1–2, I was the standard "good child" in teachers' eyes, the "other people's perfect child" in other parents' mouths. Thinking back, mostly because I loved reading as a kid and had a mountain of old knowledge to burn 🔥🔥🔥. But in grade 3, on my first study-trip base, a huge change suddenly happened (yes, a huge one): I was the dorm leader, eight of us in one room, a common sleeping platform. For the first time I felt something off: "they're all boys" — so why am I so uncomfortable? Because of that discomfort, I didn't shower once in five days 😱 I showered daily at home. !

Around day three, someone disliked my crying over everything (true — I cried at the slightest thing, but I had my pride, so I'd stop at the first tear 🤔) and said my personality was so "feminine". A mysterious feeling arose; I suddenly thought — maybe it really is. So I added a character to the Baidu Baike nickname, turning it into "Duniang" (a feminized version). What meaning do children's random nicknames have. On the last day there was a bead-stringing session: plastic beads of all colours, all cheap quality. For the first time in my life I genuinely felt pink was beautiful — before that I had dismissed pink out of stereotype. So I strung an all-pink bracelet (？

From that time on, I suddenly became like a different person: for instance, the whole "great man" thing started feeling almost mystical, and I began to like things considered "only for girls" — but without strong discomfort, just a vague sensation. Of course it would explode later — everyone knows that. From the summer after grade 3, I grew convinced I should be a girl. Just like that. Then I began thinking about it, long and hard, and concluded — seems right 😧. And then, not knowing anything, I told my parents. They probably didn't take it seriously.

Grade 4 was just constant dizziness — mysterious somatization. I was pretty happy though?! What's the mechanism?

Okay, here's the big one. During the winter break between grades 5–6, I saw a video on Bilibili someone had reposted — a coming-out video posted by ivorycello. After watching, I felt deep resonance, and from then on I discovered I was MtF. Mysterious, right (totally). That video had some serious aftereffects. After watching, I cried for a million years and visibly sank; within days I fell into a mysterious depression and began the self-harm 🔪🔪🔪 (? What meaning). The following months were the darkest of my life so far.

Winter break was short; school started. I was a bit chubby then, so I had a little chest — and got a nickname again. But this time it didn't stop at nicknames; things escalated. Some people started attacking me with malicious words — calling me a sissy, saying I had no manliness; although not a fatal attack for me, it was still ugly to hear. Before the spring sports meet, the teacher required everyone to wear short sleeves. I was in tears within moments. The big barcode on my arm could no longer be hidden. The teacher saw it and told my parents. That night when I got home, my mother burst into catastrophic crying, asking why I hurt myself. After the two of us had cried a catastrophic crying together, I told her again, seriously. They froze. After a while, my mother gave me a hug and said she didn't care whether I was a boy or a girl — as long as I was happy. Hehe, really? How could I believe that?

It happened to be time for the second study-trip base; I asked for leave, and they agreed. Dodged that bullet. The first day back at school, someone spread a rumor: that I'd caught an "STD" (the original uses a star-and-ice pun, 星冰≈性病) and stayed home so as not to infect others. I was like — a thunderous question mark. In the end I took a three-month leave of absence, all the way to the day before final exams. During those three months at home, my parents found me mysterious psychological counselling and made the trip to Guangzhou once a week — a waste of money. After that, they prescribed sertraline, which I took until grade 7. Then a whole round of operations, but in the end I never got a diagnosis, and nothing else happened — just ongoing medication to suppress anxiety and depression. Yes, at that point I was a fifth-grader already experiencing GD, hehe. During this period I attempted suicide three times — none successful. But one thing I should mention: that semester's final exam, I was second in the class and third in the grade () 

In grade 6, I couldn't take it anymore: I came out at school (am I an idiot? ). There was a mysterious person whose parents' divorce had broken their mind; they hung around school doing nothing while their family wouldn't request leave... She said she liked me, then proceeded to feed me sertraline (???), then made many attempts to hurt people (all failed), and was finally locked up in a psych ward. After that sixth grade was relatively stable — except for an event where three people tricked me to the playground and stripped off my shirt. Thinking back, quite something, too.

Side quest time. I was pretty headstrong then: online, I played it straight — fully open about my MtF identity within the "wall" (domestic internet), and I haunted some game's PvP circles. Who knows whom I'd offended, or if it was plain discrimination — my "household registration" was flying all over the internet again, haha.

In grade 7, my parents believed the psychologist had "cured" me, dropped the medication, and the mess started again. Afterwards, I seriously told them: don't call me son, I'm a girl. To this day they've only stopped calling me son — without ever seeing me as a girl. Another winter break later, mental problems resurfaced; another leave; Guangzhou again; waste money again; stopped again. But this time it wasn't because my state improved — I was just performing. I felt it cost too much money, so I learned to act like a "normal person". Finally, in the winter break between grade 8's halves, mental illness erupted: I began dissociating, spending the entire break plus the entire first semester in extreme pain and GD. I once fantasized about an accident that would force me to have surgery so I could become a girl. I more than once imagined leaping from a rooftop, ending this small half-life full of misfortune.

My family's relationships weren't healthy. My father was emotionally unstable; my mother, because my grandmother fell ill early, had been caring for her own mother since age 10 — leaving her desperately short of love and entirely without a "girlhood". Then severe role inversion appeared in the household: I was forced to absorb everyone's bad moods and soothe my mother, apologizing whether I was wrong or right, just to keep the family stable. My parents made me carry equal duties from a very young age — everything except money. But I had no equal rights: no voice, no right to ask. My needs could be ignored at will. Gradually I stopped voicing them — after all, they'd never be fulfilled; why risk being scolded for expressing needs? In elementary school I got only 30 yuan a week. How did I survive?... After middle school I had a 100-yuan weekly allowance; finally rich. But the messed-up financial mindset I'd formed in elementary school still influences me.

In the second semester of grade 8 (the spring), I made some good friends — without them, truly, I might not exist now. It all began with me getting into pjsk (Project Sekai). Then I met a group of people who loved music just as much — all very kind people. At first, exactly four of us, so we formed a "25" group, and I was "mzk" (？), so everyone started calling me Amia. I rather like that nickname, since I am a "tang tui" — a fan of Mizuki (nicknamed "Tang" in the community) () Chatting with them was delightful — but at the time, in their eyes, I was just a boy who wasn't very "masculine", so in order to keep the relationship stable, I could only maintain a sense of distance. Watching their close interactions, I was so envious. Why wasn't I born like that? One night, when I was somewhat out of it, I told a few of them my story. Fortunately — they're all truly kind people.

And then — this summer! I think I might be among the youngest of the story writers here () I've already started saving money for HRT. I still feel it's a bit late. Even though 14 is already allowed, my development came way too early. Right after my 14th birthday, my height had already settled. In the ten months since, I've grown not at all (almost) — fortunately it stopped, but it's still a bit tall. Ah... if only I had been braver before, if only I had started HRT earlier...

In any case, I've spent these five years in confusion and anxiety — until this winter break, when I confirmed that I am MtF. Before that I kept doubting and draining myself over my identity; now I'm truly certain. I genuinely cannot write down the truest thoughts in my heart; whatever I write always comes out scrambled. I really wanted to turn this piece into a warm or touching little story — but I can't. This is all I can write. Sorry～
