I still remember being in kindergarten, staring blankly at the other girls' little dresses. One day during nap time, a boy asked if I dared to wear a dress — and he really helped me borrow one from a girl. I put it on joyfully; the moment the skirt flared, I touched for the first time in this world a dream named "myself". After I woke from the dream, looking at the clothes in the wardrobe that belonged to a "boy", that longing I dared not speak of squeezed my chest till it ached. Until one summer day five years ago, I happened to click into the "crossdress masters forum". In that moment, it was like a ray of light piercing into the deep sea — I knew I wasn't a monster, and that there were others in this world searching for the light like me, meow…

In middle school, this body I dreaded grew breasts. That night, I wrapped myself in the blanket and felt the gentle rise of my chest with my palm. Even though me, the cat, understood nothing about medicine or transgender back then, and knew it might look "abnormal" to others — it was the very softest tenderness and miracle this shell that had always made me suffer had ever given me, in this cold world, yay.

![Screenshot_2026-05-10-04-38-17-811_com.hanweb.android.zhejiang.activity-edit](/pic/stories/45648863-02.jpg)

Around then, I was lucky enough to hear about LGBT and learned that in this vast world there were so many people walking bravely ahead just to be themselves. In that hidden corner called Telegram, I quietly added the blue, pink and white colours to my nickname. Those three little colour blocks, though faint, were my silent declaration to myself.

Later, the "femboy" trend rose on Chinese internet. Watching those kindred souls showing their softness and cuteness on screen, my suppressed heart stirred again. Riding that wave, I finally gathered enough courage to quietly buy myself a simple pleated skirt.

Until last year, when I met a very gentle sister in all my confusion — the cat took that fateful step at last. I still remember that cloudy day, palms drenched in sweat. Carefully, from that pink box filled with the colour of hope, I pried out the first little yellow candy — Progynova — and swallowed it with warm water… And now, time slips by, and the cat applies clear estradiol gel to my skin every day with practiced ease, feeling the coolness melt into my blood; that cyproterone (CPA) I once found terrifying, the thing I couldn't even bear to look at, has become an ordinary daily object lying quietly on the corner of the desk, keeping the cat company 💊.

![1778362141415](/pic/stories/45648863-03.jpg)

But, if only, I wish this were just a beautiful fairytale. Yet this long road really isn't a fairytale scattered with rose petals, meow… 💦

Going to the hospital for treatment, I was deeply stung by those appraising, stunned, even malicious gazes in the lobby; midway, I even ran into the most disgusting kind of creepy old man giving off the stench of sexual repression online… At that moment the cat was so scared, so close to retching, feeling as if I were being held down in a foul-smelling drain, and could only curl up in a corner…

In this circle full of hidden corners, I've also witnessed far too much blood and tears along other people's journeys. Watching on my social media feed the sisters who were thrown out of their homes after their families found out, or who mentally collapsed after running out of medication, the cat's heart is filled with deep sympathy — but more than that, a bone-chilling fear like the winter night wind😭. It's a Damoclean sword suspended overhead. Every night, the cat shrinks into a tiny ball, shivering under the covers, tears soaking through the pillow. Crying, I'd think: the cat's family conditions here are pretty ordinary by comparison — could the next one cruelly ground to dust by this ruthless world be silly me? I'm so, so tired; several times I almost couldn't hold on. The dark voice in my heart kept tempting: just give up… 💔
