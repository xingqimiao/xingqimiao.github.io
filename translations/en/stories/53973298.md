Every time I remember that my parents do love me, and that I turned myself into a "freak", the pain is unbearable. I don't want to suffer and I don't want them to suffer; those two states contradict each other, and I can only pick one while trying not to make the other worse.

## Who I was

1. Since childhood I assumed I was a twisted pervert inside.
2. I was the good student every family in the area held up as an example.
3. I was the hard-working kid who could help out at home.
4. I was the kid who won a "most filial child" certificate in middle school.
5. I was the obedient child who never had a rebellious phase.

## The struggle

I hardly touched a phone growing up, and when I did it was only to play games — mostly Genshin Impact. Before university I was certain I was a **pervert**. I assumed the feeling would disappear once I was free. I started wearing women's clothes at twelve or thirteen and had no idea why; it just felt good, deeply satisfying, so I filed myself under pervert, crossdresser.

Then I got to university, got a phone of my own, and searched. Step by step — Tieba, Zhihu — I read everything I could find, trying to work out what was wrong with me, and reached the conclusion "I want to change sex". I had never heard the word transgender. I knew **freak** and **pill girl**. So I decided that was what I wanted to be: a pill girl, a freak. I had always found freaks disgusting — dirty people, a pack of lunatics, perverts, unfilial.

Around then I learned that oestrogen could ease the distress in my body and my head. But **every medicine is partly poison**: I was scared of the side effects of taking it carelessly, and of those "**terrifying**" irreversible changes. And yet I felt so awful, and I had finally found a lifeline — why not try? I wanted to see whether it would ease the anxiety. So I bought **Progynova** and **spironolactone** myself. In October 2022 I took the first step toward myself. I had no idea what I would become; I imagined plenty of bad endings, because most freaks come to no good. But at that moment, at least, I was happy.

After a while on it the anxiety eased. First-year clubs meant a lot of socialising, so I convinced myself I had come out of it, that my illness was cured. During that period I called home every week. My parents cared about me, couldn't let me go, and gave me whatever I needed. I asked for a computer costing over seven thousand yuan — looking back, I never needed anything that expensive, but I didn't know better then. We are not a wealthy family; just ordinary farmers in Jiangxi. I had no idea how hard seven thousand yuan is to earn. I only knew that the moment I said I wanted a computer, they transferred the money. I know what that is: one way of expressing **love**. Nobody in my family is good with words. We barely talk to each other. Love is shown through action. We say the opposite of what we mean, our mouths are always sharp — every man in my family is like that.

I knew perfectly well that my parents loved me, and I was doing the thing that would end their family line. **Filial duty comes first** — since ancient times that has been the first test of whether a person is good. I cursed myself inwardly: "You really are a piece of work." I didn't want my parents to suffer, and my anxiety had eased a lot, so I searched online for how to stop the medication with the fewest side effects, and whether fertility could recover.

**Early 2023 — my first struggle. Filial duty won.**

About a month and a half later the anxiety came back, because that thing down there is so annoying, so disgusting. How to deal with it? **Medication.** Right — of everything I could think of, medication was the only thing I could afford. Last time I had swapped spironolactone for domestic oestradiol, because I didn't want to be a **pee girl**. And there I was again, swinging between taking it and not. I still loved my parents, I still wanted to be a good son to them, but… I couldn't do it. I felt awful. I was so anxious. I wanted to cut the thing off. I didn't want to be a boy. Why did I have to become this thing that is neither human nor ghost? I hated myself. I wanted to live properly like **normal** people, instead of living under the rule of male sex characteristics every day.

So I chose medication again. I bought **Estrofem** to go with the oestradiol I hadn't finished, and told myself this had to be the "**healthiest**" combination — I didn't want to end up with a pile of illnesses and not live to thirty, because then I'd have failed both myself and my parents. Wrecking my body like this was probably the worst kind of impiety. But I felt terrible, so I took it. Months passed, exactly as before, and I gave up again. I was cured, I was normal, I no longer felt anxious about what was down there. I still felt I had let my parents down. For a while I checked, every day, whether the changes were reversible — and found they were. I stopped again, and bounced along for another two months.

**June 2023 — my second struggle. Filial duty won.**

Boredom felt like falling into an infinite castle. In August I couldn't take it any more and started again. Same story: early 2024, third time stopping, still feeling I had wronged my parents.

**January 2024 — my third struggle. Filial duty won.**

Fate kept turning, and from that moment my fate slid into a whirlpool that could not end well. In February I bought injectable oestrogen, and from then on I have been on HRT continuously, to this day.

I understood something: the reason I always gave up during winter and summer holidays was that I had gone home, and felt the truest love. I think there are only four things in this life worth anything — my parents, myself, a partner, a child. I could not abandon my parents; raising me was not easy, and my life at home was good. So I wavered on the "myself" option. I could not accept being an unfilial person; the books I read told me no, my values told me no. That is why I stopped three times.

This time I was going to face my real self. I cannot ease the anxiety by anything other than medication and surgery. I think you have to look after yourself first before you can look after anything else. Since **I will die if I don't take it**, I will keep taking it. Get myself in order first. I don't think filial duty has to mean marrying and having children; it can mean seeing my parents out of this world with dignity, with fewer regrets. What they worry about most is me, their **little son**. So what they most want to see is me doing well and being happy. Marriage and children are just the line most people walk. If I look after myself I can still support them in old age — I'll have more time and money for them, because I won't be marrying. I even considered another route: find a "good friend" to share life with. "That's what all the kids who won't marry do now." I think that's completely fine. I had just been tangled up in the old tradition. This time, put myself first — and I won.

**January 2024 — my fourth struggle. My victory.**

And now, August 2026, even after the coming-out that failed, I still believe they love me. They are just rigid in their thinking, stiff in how they reason — but loving me is written into their **source code**. I think that after some time to digest it, they will be able to accept me. They tell me all the time that I do nothing and I'm useless, but I still want to say: as long as they are normal parents, what parent doesn't love their child? And as long as a child has had a proper education, what child doesn't love their parents?

Love runs both ways. Our parents need time to accept us. Let's wait for them too.

***My dad loves me, and shows it in what he does.***

***My mum loves me, and shows it in what she says.***
