## My Childhood
I was born in a very remote little village in Henan. It was poor and not open at all — maybe only slightly better than somewhere deep in the mountains, but Henan itself has countless invisible mountains pressing on people's hearts.

When I was little, my parents went out to earn money and left me at my grandparents' place. My grandparents treated me well.

I have to tell you about my grandfather. He looked very old, his face full of the marks of years. He often told me stories from his childhood — stealing melons from other people's fields, catching crickets. I loved listening to his stories. He loved teaching me to read, and whenever I met a character I didn't know, I always asked him...

As a child I liked sitting in the little room at my grandparents' house, watching the hens outside pecking at bugs, watching the sheep quietly grazing. I also liked watching the big phoenix tree in front of the door — the ants climbing on it, the hens straining to fly up it (there was no chicken coop or nest, so every evening the hens would fly up into that phoenix tree and use its thick branches as their bed).

## My Family
Both my parents were busy basically all the time, because farming in the village doesn't bring in any income at all. All four elderly people in my family were ill and needed all kinds of medicine, which dragged our finances down badly.

My mother has a terrible temper; when she was angry she would smash and kick things around. But my father is truly a remarkable person — I really admire him. He carried the weight of the whole family. He basically never got angry with my mother and never fought with her; he would just wait for her to vent and then comfort her. So although arguments were common at home, there were rarely any real conflicts.

My mother often took her bottled-up anger out on me — scolding me for this or that, comparing me to other people's kids, saying I was always lying in bed like a useless person, saying: "Such an ungrateful child, how can you be so unfilial?" Anyway, she was very naggy, but after getting used to it I mostly just let it go in one ear and out the other.

## My Life
I'd say my living conditions weren't good. I never had a bedroom of my own; I grew up in a house smaller than a public toilet for over ten years, basically always sleeping in the kitchen or on the floor. I never used a shower stall — bathing meant boiling water on a fire and washing in a big iron basin. I never ate any great delicacies; the big banquet feasts at village weddings and funerals were the best food I ever had.

I often had to help with farm work — carrying watermelons, digging sweet potatoes, peeling garlic, drying wheat, fertilizing the corn, and so on. I don't think of myself as hard-working, because I was always slacking off (it was too hot!). My parents often used that as a reason to warn me to study hard — but this kind of life wasn't something I chose!

## My Story
In elementary school I had a few playmates, but according to most people, my personality was strange — now that I think about it, it's probably because I was far too introverted! I loved studying niche games — at the time I was really into Minecraft and PVZ — but what's available on a phone is never rich enough, and I envied those streamers who played on PCs. Since elementary school I had dreamed of owning my own computer, but that was obviously a fantasy — with my family's means it was utterly impossible. So I used my father's leftover 16GB Honor phone (my father was scammed by a phone shop; that piece-of-junk phone cost 900 yuan and even calls kept freezing) and researched how to play Java Edition Minecraft on a phone plus all kinds of modded PVZ. That trained my search skills really well.

At 11 (sixth grade) I started puberty. When I saw that part of myself getting hard, I felt a vague, inexplicable disgust. But I didn't pay any attention to it, because at the time I naively really believed the "elementary-to-middle-school exam" was very important (and what good did those practice papers do? My parents bought me so many of them 🙃).

Seventh grade coursework was really simple; I felt no urgency or pressure at all (I only thought it was ridiculous to wake at 5:30 every morning and run half an hour — completely useless). So I had more time to immerse myself in the virtual world. I loved reading manga, then moved from manga to anime, and became a true "otaku" (two-dimensional person). I really liked slice-of-life shows, although that "everyday" was an everyday I could never actually experience. Seeing the cute girls in those shows didn't give me a crush — it was a strange, inexplicable feeling I couldn't articulate. Once, while scrolling Bilibili, I noticed an ad — I remember it looked like blurbs for a bunch of gender-bender novels. After reading it, that strange feeling surged up crazily. I was so curious that I downloaded the app "菠萝包轻小说" (Pineapple Bun Light Novel) and read those web novels; they felt so weird, but I couldn't stop myself from continuing. At the time I really thought I was some kind of pervert.

I would often sneak glances at female classmates, but only envying their long hair, envying their cute hair accessories. I truly didn't know what was wrong with me, but I couldn't help looking. I often drifted off in class, losing myself in fantasy — imagining that by some mysterious reason and accident I turned into a girl, and the life I'd experience after turning into a girl, and such. I had no idea why I thought this way, but I just did.

At 13 (eighth grade), after much deep thought, I told myself this might be a normal puberty phenomenon, probably because my mind and worldview were not yet fully formed — as an adolescent boy, longing for girls was perfectly normal. I used a set of airtight logic to explain everything to myself. But that was completely useless. No matter how I forced or persuaded myself, the feeling never faded. Every day I drifted off in class; I couldn't concentrate on anything, and I couldn't control myself from thinking about those things in my head. I was practically going insane. Even now I think it was almost an obsession. I often gagged from anxiety and unease, but I just thought I had a weak stomach.

I absolutely hated getting erections — a strong discomfort would sweep through my whole body. Normally I wouldn't notice the thing at all, but it just loved to pop up and demand attention. Every erection gave me extreme revulsion and disgust; I wanted to snap it off — as if it wasn't part of my body.

In the second half of eighth grade, the symptoms became worse. I was anxious every day and couldn't sleep; in class I kept drifting off and stopped understanding lessons, so my grades dropped a lot. I only remember being in pain and miserable, not knowing what was wrong with me. I just thought I was a pervert and a mental patient. My set of excuses was completely useless now, because I'd never seen any boy like me — they were all cheerful and lively, while I was becoming more and more wrong.

One weekend I truly couldn't take it anymore — I desperately wanted to cut off what was down there. I hurt myself with scissors and it bled. I'd never seen such a scene and I've always been afraid of blood, so fear took over my mind and I hurriedly cleaned all the blood and tried to stop the bleeding. Luckily it stopped, and my parents never noticed anything odd. After that I felt even worse. I didn't dare try "self-mutilation" again, but that feeling kept tormenting me. Every day, countless times, I fell uncontrollably into fantasy, because fantasy truly let me find a rare bit of comfort and peace in those illusory, beautiful stories.

Once, while looking up things in the browser, I had the random thought: I could search for information to help myself. So I searched directly: "psychological phenomena in puberty", "what are the phenomena of pubertal development". Yes — I was still brainwashing myself. I still thought it was a puberty phenomenon; I truly believed everyone had the same thoughts as me, that everyone felt anxious about their own bodily development. During that period I kept deceiving myself — even though at the time I myself didn't believe puberty could cause this kind of unexplained harm. The result: I found no useful information at all.

Two weeks later, it hit me to search: "Is often fantasizing about becoming another gender a mental illness?" (Why did I love such long, awkward sentences when I was little?) And I found a marketing post about the "yaoniang" (trans women on hormones) group. After reading it I was terrified: why do these people do this like this? Isn't this just being a "shemale"? Even though I was scared, I secretly searched the term "yaoniang" too. This time I found many posts — I learned why they eat "sugar" (take HRT), and saw their side that the world doesn't understand. Comparing with my own experience, I found a lot of real similarities. I wanted to get closer to them, so I joined a "yaoniang" forum I'd found. I saw people sharing their experiences there — their internal struggles, and the joy of their bodies changing. But I was afraid it was an abyss with no return once entered, a drug addiction that could never be quit, so I hid the forum, only daring to peek in late at night when everyone was asleep.

After that I grew fine whiskers and an Adam's apple. I plucked out every single whisker and pressed hard on my Adam's apple to make it disappear, sometimes choking myself until I vomited before stopping. After seeing the effects of the "sugar" those sisters posted, I always wanted to eat some, but I feared "medicine is three-parts poison" — some enormous side effect — and worried what would happen if I was found out, whether I'd eat myself to death, and so on. I didn't dare touch it at all. Long-term anxiety twisted me a bit, and I started trying more extreme methods to make myself "normal": I bought a pile of cheap small blades and began "fighting poison with poison". At first it worked — my attention was completely drawn to the wounds, so basically no more wild fantasies. But after one or two times I realized it was still completely useless. I truly broke down; I had no idea and no understanding of why I had become like this.

In the end, out of options, with the resolve of a "certain death", I decided to use the 100 yuan New Year money I'd secretly saved to buy "sugar". And I really did buy five boxes of domestic *Progynova* from a dealer (there were tons of scammers on the forum back then — not getting scammed was really great luck)!! But when they arrived I had no idea how to take them, so I joined the QQ group shared on the forum and asked all kinds of questions. A very kind sister helped answer, but then a moderator came, judged me a "kid with skewed values", cursed me out, and kicked me. I could only ask for help inside the forum, but every time I got called "stupid brat" or "dead 郭楠 (Chinese male), stop coming here for attention". I couldn't dare speak anymore, so I secretly rejoined the QQ group and slowly rummaged through the group files myself.

I finally did find a useful document. When I opened and read it, I found it was truly detailed, so I treasured it like a gem — studying it seriously every night, even reviewing parts I'd forgotten. I learned about the effects of various drugs and the damage they might do to the body, and at last I learned what transgender actually means. I immersed myself in it every day, imagining the changes those medicines would bring to my body, feeling inexplicably happy inside. I carried one box of domestic Progynova with me and hid the other four in the deepest part of the cupboard. For nearly a month, the masculine development I hated had stalled. I was thrilled when I noticed, but also felt deeply unsettled: I'm letting my parents down by secretly taking this medicine; what if I become sterile later? What if an outsider finds out and it gets around — how ugly would that be... Yet I couldn't make myself stop, because the effect of those drugs made me feel like I was finally being myself.

One day after school, the moment I got home, my father pulled out four boxes of *Progynova* and questioned me sternly: "What is this?" I was so scared I stammered, then lied that a girl classmate had thrown them away and I'd taken them to play with. But does anyone believe a lie that cheap? I had no choice but to tell him everything I'd been hiding. He seemed shocked — he said only "I didn't even do this when I was a boy" — and since we both had school the next day, we went to bed early. But neither of us slept a wink.

Later, my father thought I might have depression from the pressure of studying, so he took me to the city's psychiatric hospital. The result: severe depression, and a pile of psychiatric drugs. Every time I took those pills I got dizzy and could barely think; I was afraid of that feeling, so I kept skipping them. Out of options, my father took me to the big Zhengzhou hospital (very "parent-arranged treatment" vibes). I still remember that doctor — although he didn't understand transgender, he was genuinely kind; he helped defuse both our emotions and spoke warmly. In the end, he prescribed a few boxes of sertraline. My father, seeing how poor my state was, applied for a six-month leave of absence from school.

During my leave he kept trying to find out what illness I had. To my surprise, he stumbled on Pan Bailin's videos somehow, and suggested taking me to Peking University Third Hospital. I was truly stunned! But Pan Bailin's appointment was too hard to get, so all we could get was Chang Xu's. After much fussing I got my little certificate (a useless scrap of paper). After that my parents stopped interfering and never brought it up again — but they've always thought I'd get better once I grew up. I hoped so too — I'd really get better. Now it looks like that hope is fading 😖

## My Attitude
Truthfully, from the beginning I've been a pessimist, living each day with negative thoughts. I'm not even a good person. I've told the certificate story simply and quickly, but at the very start my parents spent all day making snide remarks — saying I was addicted to the medicine, that any little oddity in me was caused by the medicine! I once held extremely negative thoughts of suicide, but I also hated this world so much that the twisted version of me wanted to drag a few people "as company" to the grave before dying — so I went on X and crazily attacked other trans people. After three days of madness my account was finally banned. Only then did I start reflecting. Before that I also thought: life is this rotten, might as well die. During my leave I lay in bed giving up on everything, doing nothing but scrolling my phone and insulting people. I copied others and started OD-ing as self-harm, knocking myself out of my mind every day — at the time I really was neither human nor ghost. After that reflection I finally saw what I was doing. I sent apology letters to everyone I had attacked and quit the internet. Then I threw away all the medicine and blades — but I still kept the giving-up lifestyle, scrolling videos every day.

Once I ran into a video by the Bilibili uploader Shaxin Chengfen Jiang about surviving as a homeless man, and after reading the comments, I learned that this really is how some people live. I thought: if a person's life has come to this, why not just die? But the optimism that uploader showed — that tenacity of giving everything just to keep living — truly moved me deeply. For the first time I felt that living itself is also magnificent. Early death and late death are both death, so why not keep living and see what tomorrow brings? I never chase grand dreams — I can't achieve them, and there's no need to add extra pressure to myself. Right now I only want to live in each present moment, and fight against this rotten, garbage world to the last second!!! (I'm quite the chuuni 😋)

## My Future
The phoenix tree in my grandmother's yard was cut down long ago, yet the old roots still grow new sprouts. Yes — as long as you keep living, you can always put out a new shoot! After wind and rain, not necessarily a rainbow — but the rain always stops, and the sky always clears. The future is like the weather: unpredictable, full of unknowns.

People are the same — like regions of land, each with its own unique climate, yet even so, every region has its own clear day. More or less, keep going and you'll always see it! So keep walking on, strong and steady — let's see what good weather tomorrow brings?
