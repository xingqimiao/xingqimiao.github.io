---
title: The Girl Who Stole Lipstick Has Finally Grown Up
---

Counting from age five, when my mother's words "raise her as a girl" stirred waves in my heart that wouldn't quiet for ages — it's been over a decade now. Unfortunately, most of that time I spent papering over the peace with the argument that "a normal person placed into the wrong-sex body doesn't necessarily have to pursue their original gender identity by 'remodeling the body'." If the environment had truly been ideal, perhaps I wouldn't have needed from elementary school to university — ten years of careful deliberation. I owe an apology to the me of first grade who secretly used lipstick; to the me of third grade who grieved that the school skirts didn't belong to me; to the me of sixth grade who loved gender-bender/gender-switch fiction. I sympathize with the me of middle school who acted like a "normal boy" and made many good friends; with the me of high school who suppressed that "difference" and even fought with my parents — who had already given so much for me — over the right to commute day-school; and with the me whose college entrance exam failed due to illness, starting university after a year of leave. I am also comforted by the me who, in the end, found all of myself.
