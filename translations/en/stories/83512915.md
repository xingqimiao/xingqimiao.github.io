Long time no see. Old Me speaking — the me I hid deep in my heart.

Transgender isn't actually the identity, or "illness", that was hardest for me to accept. My life has been rich, and I'll tell it slowly:

## Transgender

I first became conscious around age 12: watching the girls in my class develop, I would fantasize that I was a girl too. At 13 my body started developing. I had never liked my genitals; when they began developing, the thing's existence grew stronger and stronger. Every night I wanted to remove it — snapping and bending it until it hurt to the cartilage. Having seen "cut it off with scissors" on TV, I tried many times, but never dared. A few times I cut the skin and it hurt so much I stopped; once it actually bled, but not deep, not much. I also taped it down so it wouldn't get hard, so it wouldn't look conspicuous — that helped a bit. I knew all of this was harmful to my body, but I couldn't take it; I couldn't control myself; it was like my instinct to resist the thing. I envied those girls and wanted to become one. I didn't know what was wrong with me. I was a pervert, a weirdo, mentally ill. I kept fed-exing these ideas to myself over and over: maybe it'll get better later; maybe this is just adolescent curiosity about the opposite sex — I hoped so.

My 12-to-18 years were nearly identical, unremarkable. During my middle-school years: at 13, cross-dressing; at 14, an MP4 for reading novels — gender-bender stories, sex-swap stories — projecting myself in, which gave me inner satisfaction for a while. In my spare time I read novels; before sleep I'd write myself a novel in my head, the protagonist being me, fantasizing I was a girl born into various environments, playing out her life from birth to adulthood (since I didn't know what came after adulthood then). Every night these fantasies held me up like a spiritual pillar; only around 20 did they slowly fade. As a child I secretly wore girls' clothes and was discovered — but was ignored, time and again...

My turning point was 18, the year I entered university. I had a phone; I was a relatively independent person; I could do some of the things I wanted. Because I had little contact with phones before, I never knew about transgender or similar concepts; oestrogen being the dominant female hormone was something I learned in biology. I searched the browser: "can boys take oestrogen", then "boys taking oestrogen", "a boy who wants to become a girl", "shemale", "transsexual", "what drugs do transsexuals take"... step by step, until the browser pushed posts from Tieba and Zhihu, where I learned the concept of transgender — and found a cute forum. Then I bought medicine online with a feminine-identity card: domestic Progynova (guobu) and spironolactone. Eat, eat, eat. Because spironolactone made me a "little pee-girl", I switched to domestic cyproterone (guose). The year-and-a-half from 18 to 19 was my self-exploration phase. During it I went on Twitter and Telegram — but only to see what medications existed; I also tried gel, Bica and similar. In that period I stopped the medication several times, each time feeling cured, having become a normal person — then a while later, because that thing was too disgusting, I went back on it. I kept searching Twitter with keywords like "self-surgery" and "black-market surgery"; fear plus no money kept me from going through with it. Back then things were indeed plentiful, and medication was cheaper than now. But I didn't know how to use Twitter, so I never really got into the trans community. I kept believing I was an extremely rare case, so I let it be.

During this whole period I fought against my own heart, ethics, and morality: is this being irresponsible to myself? Is this against human ethics? Is this letting my parents down? etc... But I had no other options — gender unease was too strong. Starting at 20, I began taking injections continuously — before I knew it, two and a half years passed, until last month when I switched medications.

My entry into the "trans community" happened in March this year, second semester of senior year. With nothing to do, I wanted to check out X, so I started posting. At first nobody read; gradually I posted more and more, and the trans people I encountered multiplied. The gear of fate began turning from here. In early April I went to get my "little certificate" — except I didn't know it was a little certificate, because as soon as I spoke, they wrote "gender dysphoria" on it. This period was mainly poor mental state, so I went on X; I saw people taking PR80 (pregabalin) to sleep well, and since I was only sleeping about three hours a night, I followed the trend and took it — instead of getting a prescription from the hospital (which was my misstep). That's a stain on my record. Seeing fellow users' tragic experiences, thinking of my own tragic life, I copied their OD self-harm and suicide attempts. In truth I've always been a big, sunny boy; receiving too many negative emotions at once, I couldn't digest them all... Later, thinking about my messed-up life, I took salt for suicide. Hilarious — if I could talk to that me, I'd laugh my head off. Mainly the failed coming-out hurt too much. Afterwards, my mother decided she didn't want me to die, so I seized the moment to make demands and got directly the "big certificate". Beautiful. Afterwards, I became an anti-trans person on X... I don't know why they treated me like that — I hadn't hurt anyone; I just liked insulting people and opposing trans...

Words cannot express my persistence. After finishing, it feels like nothing; I can't write out that pain.

## The Rest

I'm a disabled trans person.

I come from a family that favors girls over boys: my sister was raised like an elegant young lady of the city, while I was practically a wild child. My mother has a pathological level of controlling instinct — the strongest controller I've met, including the online "gods of control". You can imagine the controlling behaviors and speech, she could do them all: only because I'm older now, and harder to handle than before. And as the most learned member of my family, everything I say gets denied.

I have many illnesses myself; the big ones:
1. Hereditary retinal degeneration — my vision is already disabled.
2. Congenital heart disease — crossed out, didn't kill me.
3. A few basic chronic diseases that torment people.

The inconvenience from family and body bothers me more than gender — especially my eyes. With a pair of good eyes, I wouldn't be living like a maggot the way I am now. Because the focus is transgender-related, I won't write about these experiences — the vision issue is too painful.

I'm about to enter graduate school, so my life doesn't end up too badly... I've seen many tragic cases, but none were more tragic than mine, and none more optimistic than mine — even though my reputation in the community is terrible.
